# September polish pass

This pass retains the island layout, tank movement and double jump, with a clearer play view and less vegetation overhead.
The visual reference remains [Astro’s Playroom](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/astros-playroom/).

## Changes

- Camera starts at a lower pitch and restores the gameplay field of view after a directed scene.
- Camera obstruction uses a swept sphere, including a second check after smoothing, and ignores sensors and the player body.
- The daylight key is less yellow, the horizon is bluer, the Core emits more strongly, and the vignette is lighter.
- Grass root shading has a narrower contrast range.
- High quality uses 120,000 grass blades and 6,500 flowers, previously 220,000 and 14,000.
- Medium quality uses 55,000 grass blades and 3,000 flowers, previously 70,000 and 4,500.
- The HUD has location branding, completion segments, optional controls, and a contextual lesson card.
- The lesson button uses the same action as the keyboard, including entering and replaying the playable lesson.
- HUD button keyboard input no longer also triggers gameplay actions.

## Verification notes

Four Rapier integration tests cover open space, near-plane edge clearance, close walls and excluded colliders.
The texture test still checks every pixel, but collects failures before asserting instead of running tens of thousands of individual assertions.
Vitest is limited to two workers to avoid memory contention with a running WebGL preview.

Desktop and narrow-window captures verified the HUD and vegetation pass.
Browser debugger timeouts prevented a reliable final lighting capture, full traversal test and FPS comparison.
The vegetation counts are a measured workload reduction, not an FPS claim.
Earlier numerical lighting measurements in the historical design notes predate this daylight adjustment.
